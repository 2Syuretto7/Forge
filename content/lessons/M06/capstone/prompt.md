# Capstone: a growth-law detector

The starter's `make_datasets(seed)` returns six datasets `(x, y)`, each generated by one law with multiplicative noise of about 3 percent:

- **linear:** $y = a + b x$;
- **exponential:** $y = a\, b^x$;
- **power:** $y = a\, x^p$.

Write:

- `fit_line(u, v)`: `(slope, intercept, r2)` of the least-squares line (you may use `np.polyfit` for the slope and intercept; compute $R^2$ yourself).
- `classify(x, y)`: `"linear"`, `"exponential"` or `"power"`, whichever coordinate system (plain, $\ln y$ against $x$, or $\ln y$ against $\ln x$) gives the largest $R^2$.
- `estimate(x, y, law)`: the parameters as a dictionary: `{"a": ..., "b": ...}` for linear and exponential, `{"a": ..., "p": ...}` for a power law.
- `detect(datasets)`: a list with one `(law, params)` pair per dataset.

The tests check every classification and every parameter to within 5 percent. Before you run anything, look at the numbers in each dataset and guess the law: a quantity that grows by a constant factor per step, or one whose growth slows down, or neither.
