# Recall bridge: from events to numbers

M21 asked how likely events are and how learning one changes another. Most questions in machine learning are about numbers instead: the loss on a batch, the value of a weight at initialization, the count of errors in a test set. A random variable attaches a number to each outcome, and two summaries of its distribution, the mean and the variance, carry most of what you will need. Clear your due cards above first.

## What carries over

Probabilities of events are still the foundation: $P(X = 3)$ is the probability of the event "the outcome gives 3". Integrals from M17 compute probabilities when the variable is continuous, and independence from M21 is exactly what makes variances add.
