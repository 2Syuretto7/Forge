# Recall bridge: from strips to tiles

M17 integrated along a line and computed the Gaussian integral by squaring it and switching to polar coordinates, with a factor $r$ that was announced but not derived. M18 described a map between spaces by its Jacobian, the best linear map near each point. This lesson integrates over regions of the plane and of space, and derives the factor $r$ and all its relatives from the Jacobian. Clear your due cards above first.

## What carries over

M10 showed that a linear map multiplies every area by $|\det A|$. A curved map is linear on tiny scales, so it multiplies tiny areas by the determinant of its Jacobian. Substitution in one dimension, $dx = g'(t)\,dt$, was the one-dimensional case all along.
