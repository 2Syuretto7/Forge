# Recall bridge: when is a step right?

M02 ended with a checker: evaluate both sides of a claimed step at fifty random points and compare. It caught every error you planted. This lesson asks what such a check can and cannot tell you, and what a proof adds. Clear the M02 cards above first.

## What the checker did

The checker turned a claim about all numbers ("these two expressions are equal for every value of the variables") into fifty claims about particular numbers. If any of the fifty failed, the claim was false, and you held a **counterexample**: a specific case where it breaks. If all fifty passed, you had evidence.

:::

## The asymmetry

One counterexample refutes a claim about every case. No number of examples proves one. That asymmetry is the subject of this lesson. A claim can hold for the first million cases and fail on the next, and you will meet real examples of exactly that in Movement 4.

A **proof** is an argument that covers every case at once, built from steps each of which follows by a rule from the steps before. You have already written proofs without the name: the derivation of the quadratic formula in M02 is one, valid for every $a \neq 0$, $b$ and $c$ simultaneously.
