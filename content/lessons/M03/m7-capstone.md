# Capstone: a proof portfolio

Ten true statements, each with two kinds of support: a written proof that covers every case, and a machine check that hunts for counterexamples over many cases. The proofs are the point; the hunter keeps you honest about what you claim.

For each statement, write the predicate as a Python function, a proof in words in `PROOFS`, and the method you used. The tests run the hunter over each predicate and check that every proof names its method and is long enough to be an argument.

:::exercise{ref=capstone}
:::

## One proof, graded

Pick one statement from your portfolio, the one whose proof you are least sure of, and write it out in full for a reader who has seen M02 and this lesson but nothing else. The grader checks that each step follows from the previous ones.

:::feynman{id=proof1 prompt="Write a complete proof of one statement from your portfolio (for example: the square root of 2 is irrational; if n squared is even then n is even; n cubed minus n is divisible by 6). State the method you use and justify every step." key_points="States precisely what is assumed and what is to be proved|Names the method: direct, contrapositive, contradiction or cases|Each step follows from earlier steps or from a stated fact|Covers every case, not just examples|Ends by stating that the claim is proved and why the argument is complete"}
:::
